Doubling Rule -ed, -ing: Doubling rule with -ed and -ing

Instructional Notes

When a one-syllable word has one vowel followed by one consonant (a 1-1-1 word: hop, stop, run), double the final consonant before adding a suffix that starts with a vowel (-ed, -ing): hop → hopped, hopping. Doubling keeps the vowel short (hop-ping vs. ho-ping).

Do not double when the word ends in two consonants (jump → jumping) or has a vowel team (rain → rained).

5: New Concept

Introduction

We know the suffixes -ed and -ing. Today we will learn a spelling rule for adding them. Look at the word stop: s-t-o-p. It has one vowel, then one consonant at the end. We call this a 1-1-1 word: one syllable, one vowel, one final consonant.

When we add -ed or -ing to a 1-1-1 word, we double the last consonant: stop → stopped, stopping. Why? The double consonant keeps the vowel short. If we wrote 'stoping', the o would look long (sto-ping).

If the word ends in two consonants (jump) or has two vowels (rain), do not double: jumped, raining.

The Busy Day

When Edwin got home from work, he dropped his bag on the floor and plopped on the couch. What a busy day I had,” he sighed. “I am exhausted.” Edwin’s roommate walked in. “What made your day so busy?” he asked. Edwin explained. “It started this morning when I dropped off some letters at the post office. The line was so long that I ended up running late for work. Then, at work, I had so many meetings. It felt like they were never going to end. I planned to come home for lunch, but I was so busy I skipped that trip. I grabbed a sandwich and a drink from the store on the corner. As I was jogging back to the office, I tripped and dropped my drink. But I was too busy to go back and get new one. When my day was finally over, I hopped in my car and drove home.” “What are you planning to do this evening?” Edwin’s roommate asked. “Nothing!” Edwin responded, “I will be sitting right here on this couch, resting.”
