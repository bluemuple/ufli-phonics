Signal Vowels: Signal vowels: c /s/ and g /j/ before e, i, y

Instructional Notes

E, I and Y are 'signal vowels': after C they signal /s/ (cent, city, cycle) and after G they usually signal /j/ (gem, giant, gym). Before A, O, U (and consonants) C spells /k/ and G spells /g/. Exceptions for G: get, give, girl, gift, begin.

5: New Concept

Introduction

We have learned that C spells /k/ (cat) and /s/ in -ce (race), and that G spells /g/ (goat) and /j/ in -ge (cage). Today we will learn why.

The vowels E, I and Y are signal vowels. When C is followed by e, i or y, it signals the soft sound /s/: cent, city, cycle, cell. When G is followed by e, i or y, it usually signals /j/: gem, giant, gym, energy.

Before a, o, u or a consonant, C spells /k/ (cat, cot, cup, clap) and G spells /g/ (gap, got, gum, glad). A few common G words break the rule: get, give, girl, gift.

A Trip to the City

Gemma took a train trip to New York City to visit her Uncle Quincy. When she arrived, Uncle Quincy was there to greet her at the airport. “Welcome!” called Uncle Quincy with glee, “I am so excited to have you as my guest for the week. I can’t wait to be your city guide.” “I’m excited too!” replied Gemma, “I have been imagining this trip for a long time.” Their first excursion was to a fancy ice cream parlour. Gemma got a giant scoop of cinnamon ice cream. “This is incredible,” exclaimed Gemma between bites. Next, the pair walked back to Uncle Quincy’s apartment. When they got there, they saw a letter taped to the door. It was marked urgent. “I wonder what that could be,” said Uncle Quincy with a sly smile, “open it up and see if there is a message inside.” Gemma opened the letter and two tickets fell into her hands. “Surprise! I managed to get us tickets to a concert at Madison Square Garden. It was sold out, but my friend is a ticket agent. She got us the last two general admission seats.” “Wow,” gasped Gemma, “I never would have guessed I’d get to see a real concert. Thank you, Uncle Quincy. This is the best trip ever!”
