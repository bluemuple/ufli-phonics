-s/-es: The suffixes -s and -es (morphemes)

Instructional Notes

This lesson revisits -s and -es as morphemes: word parts that change meaning. -s/-es make nouns plural (one dog, two dogs) and change verbs (I run, he runs). Use -es after s, x, z, sh, ch.

5: New Concept

Introduction

A morpheme is a word part that changes a word's meaning. A suffix is a morpheme added to the end of a word. We know the suffixes -s and -es.

Nouns are people, animals, places and things: dog, shirt, box. Adding -s or -es makes a noun mean more than one: dogs, shirts, boxes. Verbs describe an action: run, brush. Adding -s or -es changes the verb: I run, she runs; I brush, he brushes.

Add -es after s, x, z, sh and ch, because -es adds a syllable that we can hear: box-es, brush-es.

Articulatory Gesture

No new sound. Review that -s says /s/ after quiet sounds and /z/ after voiced sounds.

A Trip to the Football

The boys and girls of Miss Brown’s third grade class are jumping up and down with delight when the buses pull up in front of them. All the third grade classes are going on a trip to see a football game today. After the teachers check who is present, the students and teachers make their way onto the buses and sit down. As they drive to the football ground, the students look out the windows and sing silly songs. When the bus pulls up, they go inside. They look at the numbers on the benches to find their seats. The game starts only minutes later. The students cheer as they watch the home team score the first points. The home team makes some impressive kicks, bounces, and tackles. As the game is about to end, the classes scream with joy. They see themselves on the big screen! On the ride back home, the students and teachers talk about the thrilling events of the day. They all agree they should take a trip to the football ground again soon.
