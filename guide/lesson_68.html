Open/Closed: Two-syllable words: open/closed

Instructional Notes

When one consonant sits between two vowels (V/CV), divide before the consonant: ro-bot, o-pen. The first syllable is open, so its vowel is long. If that does not make a real word, try dividing after the consonant (closed, short vowel): rob-in, cab-in. This flexibility is called 'set for variability'.

5: New Concept

Introduction

Yesterday we divided words between two consonants (rab/bit). Today we will look at words with only one consonant between the vowels, like robot: r-o-b-o-t.

When there is one consonant in the middle, we usually divide before the consonant: ro / bot. The first syllable is open, so the vowel says its name: /rō/. Then read the second syllable: /bŏt/. Blend: robot.

Sometimes dividing before the consonant does not make a real word. Then be flexible: try dividing after the consonant and make the vowel short: rob / in, robin. Try the long sound first, then the short sound.
