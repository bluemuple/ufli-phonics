-ar, -or /ə/: -ar and -or spell /ə/ (schwa) at the end of a word

Instructional Notes

In an unstressed final syllable, AR and OR are pronounced with the schwa /ə/ (the same sound as ER in letter): dollar, doctor. In Australian/NZ English these endings all sound like /ə/. Spelling requires memory: ar (dollar, collar, sugar), or (doctor, actor, visitor).

5: New Concept

Introduction

We know that AR spells /ar/ (car) and OR spells /or/ (fork). Today we will learn that at the end of a longer word, in a syllable that is not stressed, AR and OR sound different.

Listen: dollar. The second syllable is quiet and relaxed: /ə/. That is the schwa sound - the same sound as the ER at the end of letter. So at the end of a two-syllable word, -ar (dollar, collar) and -or (doctor, actor) both sound like /ə/.

Divide the word into syllables, read the first syllable, then the -ar or -or ending: doc / tor, doctor. When spelling, you have to remember whether the word uses ar, or or er.

A Pet Lizard

Have you ever considered getting a pet lizard? They may seem like an strange choice, but they can be lovely pets. Lizards are reptiles. They can be big or small and many different colours. You can create a nice habitat for your lizard to live in. Start with a big cage so your lizard has room to roam. You will also need some food for your lizard. Lizards eat bugs and worms and plants. Some even like to eat flowers. Perhaps they like the flavour of the nectar inside the flowers. Unlike dogs and cats, lizards do not need collars and do not need to be taken on walks. Just remember, if they ever get sick, take them to a doctor who can help them get better. So, what do you think? Would you want to have a pet lizard?
