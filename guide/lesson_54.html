a_e /ā/: The VCe pattern a_e spells /ā/

Instructional Notes

VCe = vowel, consonant, silent e. The silent e signals that the vowel says its long sound (its name). Teach the pattern by changing short-vowel words: cap → cape, mad → made.

Word work uses writing or magnetic letters with a separate e tile.

5: New Concept

Introduction

We have learned that the letter A spells /ă/ as in cat. Today we will learn that A can also spell /ā/, the long A sound, which is the letter's name.

Look at this word: cap. Now I add an e at the end: cape. The e at the end is silent, but it does a job: it tells the vowel to say its name. A, consonant, silent e - we call this the VCe pattern. c-a-p-e, cape. The vowel says /ā/.

When you read a word with a vowel, one consonant and an e at the end, the vowel is long: made, same, take. When you spell, remember the silent e at the end.

Articulatory Gesture

To make the /ā/ sound as in 'acorn', start with your mouth open a little and glide to a smile... (model). Be sure your voice is on. /ā/ is a continuous sound. Watch me /āāā/. You try /āāā/.

Sound Wall

Add 'a_e' under /ā/ on the vowel valley.

Decodable Text (from the slides)

Cave in the Maze

Dale and Jade went into a maze to win a game. The maze was long and full of snakes. Dale said to Jade, “I can not go on.” “Yes, you can. You are brave!” said Jade. Dale and Jade walk and talk. They made a left and got to the end of the maze. Then they see six small caves. “Let’s go into the sixth cave,” said Jade. In the back of the cave, they see a gate! The gate is shut. Are they stuck? Dale shakes the gate. Jade kicks the gate. The gate is still shut. “Look! The gate has a lock,” yells Jade. Dale twists the lock. Pop! The gate swings in. Dale and Jade win the game!
