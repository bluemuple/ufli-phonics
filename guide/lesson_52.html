n /ŋ/ (as in nk): The grapheme NK spells /ŋk/

Instructional Notes

In NK, the N spells /ŋ/ (not /n/) and the K spells /k/: pink = /p/ /ĭ/ /ŋ/ /k/. Count it as two sounds when segmenting. Some programs treat 'nk' as a unit for the visual drill, as UFLI does.

5: New Concept

Introduction

We learned that NG spells /ŋ/. Today we will look at NK. When N comes before K, the N spells /ŋ/ and the K spells /k/. So NK spells two sounds, /ŋ/ /k/, like at the end of pink.

NK comes at the end of a word, such as pink and sunk. It never comes at the beginning of a word.

Grapheme placement (slides)

pink/sunk/nk

Articulatory Gesture

Say /ŋ/ (voice through the nose, tongue back) then /k/ (quick pop): /ŋk/. Watch me /ŋk/. You try /ŋk/.

Sound Wall

Add 'nk' under /ŋ/ with a note that k follows.

Decodable Text (from the slides)

Stink in the Bank

Hank has a job at the bank. When Hank gets to the bank, he sniffs a bad smell. “What is that smell?” Hank thinks. “Is it a skunk?” He sniffs his desk. “No, it’s not that.” He checks the fish tank. “No, this is not what stinks.” He walks to the back of the bank. When he gets to the back, he sees the small bin. “Oh no!” Hank yells, “I left all the stuff in the bin.” Hank sniffs the bin. “Yuck!” he yells, “that’s what stinks!” Hank brings the small bin to the big bin and slams the lid shut. Hank is glad he got rid of the stink.
