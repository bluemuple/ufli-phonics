Drop -e Rule: Drop -e rule

Instructional Notes

When a word ends in silent e, drop the e before adding a suffix that begins with a vowel (-ed, -ing, -er, -est): hope → hoped, hoping; nice → nicer, nicest. Keep the e before a suffix that begins with a consonant (hopeful, safely).

5: New Concept

Introduction

We have learned the doubling rule. Today we will learn another spelling rule for adding suffixes: the drop -e rule.

Look at the word hope: it ends in silent e. When we add a suffix that starts with a vowel, like -ing, -ed, -er or -est, we drop the silent e first: hope → hoping, hoped. Nice → nicer, nicest. Bake → baked, baking, baker.

We drop the e because the suffix's vowel now does the silent e's job of keeping the vowel long: ho-ping. If the suffix starts with a consonant, keep the e: hopeful, safely, nicely.

Decodable Text (from the slides)

The Baker

Rashawn is a world class baker. He has been baking since he was a little boy. Every day you can find him dancing around the kitchen while making yummy treats. He also loves trading tips with other bakers about how to make their dishes even better. One day, Rashawn’s friend, Payton, came over to visit. Payton was hoping to taste some of Rashawn’s newest treats. “You’re right on time!” Rashawn called. “I was just taking a fresh pie out of the oven.” He cut two slices of pie and handed the larger slice to Payton. “I am sure you’re going to love it,” Rashawn said smiling. “Yuck!” Payton yelled as she tasted the pie. “What do you mean, yuck?” asked Rashawn, his smile fading, “You don’t like it?” “Just joking!” Payton said with a grin. “This is the best pie I have ever tasted!” “Oh good,” Rashawn sighed as he sliced the rest of the pie up. “But next time, it would be nicer to say you liked it from the start.”
