-s /s/: The suffix -s spells /s/

Instructional Notes

-s is the first suffix (morpheme) students learn. Today the words end with quiet sounds (t, p, k), so -s says /s/. Lesson 21 covers -s /z/.

Explain the meaning: adding -s to a noun means more than one (cat → cats); adding -s to a verb tells what one person or thing does (sit → sits).

5: New Concept

Introduction

Today we will learn about the suffix -s. A suffix is a word part added to the end of a word. Adding -s changes what the word means.

When we add -s to a naming word (a noun) it means more than one: one cat, two cats. When we add -s to an action word (a verb) it tells what one person does: I sit, he sits.

After the quiet sounds /t/, /p/ and /k/, the suffix -s says /s/: cats, maps.

Articulatory Gesture

Review the /s/ gesture (teeth together, quiet air).

Sound Wall

Add '-s' under /s/.
