a /ar/: The grapheme A spells /ar/ (Australian/NZ pronunciation)

Instructional Notes

This lesson has been added into the Australian scope and sequence because in Australian/NZ English the letter A commonly represents /ar/ before s, th, f and n clusters: bath, last, ask, after, dance.

This makes ask and fast (heart words since Lesson 22) fully decodable.

5: New Concept

Introduction

We have learned about the /ar/ sound. We learned AR can spell /ar/ like in the words bark, far, and arm.

The letter A can also represent the /ar/ sound. A spells /ar/ in the middle of a word, such as the words bath and last. A can spell /ar/ at the beginning of a word, such as the words ask and after.

Grapheme placement (slides)

bath/last/a ; ask/after/a

Articulatory Gesture

To make the /ar/ sound as in 'arm', start with an open relaxed mouth and as you say the sound, drop your chin a little like this (model). Be sure your voice is on. Like all vowels, /ar/ is a continuous sound that can be stretched out (use continuous hand motion). Watch me /ar/. You try /ar/.

Sound Wall

Add 'a' under /ar/.

A Flamingo Party

At half-past two, we went to the park close to home. It was full of lush, green grass and very tall trees. By the vast lake, there were pretty, pink birds. “Flamingos!” I called out. The flock moved very fast across the grass. “What are they doing?” I asked. Mum said, "It looks like they are having a party. What a blast!" They stood on one leg and used their long necks to find food. Dad told me flamingos can’t fly. Silly Dad!  Then I went to the swings and slid on the slide. “Oh no! It is time to go,” said Dad. At last, I waved to the flamingos and told them I would come back soon.
