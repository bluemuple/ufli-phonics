au, aw, augh /or/: The vowel teams AU, AW and AUGH spell /or/ (/aw/)

Instructional Notes

In Australian/NZ English, the sound in saw, haul and caught is the same as the /or/ in fork and more. The UFLI US edition calls it /aw/. AU comes in the middle of a word (haul, sauce); AW at the end of a word or before n/l (saw, lawn, crawl); AUGH before t (caught, taught).

5: New Concept

Introduction

We know that OR and ORE spell /or/ (fork, more). Today we will learn three vowel teams that spell the same sound in our accent: /or/ (in American books it is written /aw/).

AW spells /or/ at the end of a word (saw, draw, law) and before n or l (lawn, crawl). AU spells /or/ in the middle of a word (haul, sauce, August). AUGH spells /or/ before t (caught, taught, daughter) - the gh is silent.

Listen: saw, for, haul, more - the vowel sound is the same. When spelling, think about where the sound is in the word.

Trip to the Zoo

Audrey and Willow could not wait to see all the animals at the zoo. As they reached the entrance, Audrey paused and asked, “did you bring the tickets?” “Yes! I have them right here,” said Willow. The first display they visited was full of birds. “Look at that pretty macaw! Its wings are like a rainbow,” exclaimed Audrey. “I see the macaw,” responded Willow, “but look at this hawk! It caught a fish in its claws.” Willow could have spent hours at the bird display, but Audrey said she wanted to see more animals. The next display contained a lion sleeping on a large lawn. The lion woke up for five minutes, but then yawned and fell back asleep. “He seems to have day and night mixed up,” snickered Audrey. “Well, he’s not to blame. If I had to hunt for my food, I would want a nap too,” Willow commented. Audrey smirked and said, “That reminds me, I am hungry. Can we go hunt for some food at the snack stand?”
