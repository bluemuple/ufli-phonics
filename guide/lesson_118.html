Alternate ch, gn, gh, silent t: Alternate ch /sh/, /k/; gn /n/; gh /g/; silent t

Instructional Notes

The high frequency word 'often' can be pronounced with or without the /t/.

The sound CH represents is dictated by the origin of the word. CH as /ch/ is of English origin, CH as /k/ is of Greek origin, and CH as /sh/ is of French origin.

5: New Concept

Introduction

Today we will learn some less common letter-sound patterns. These patterns only happen in a few words.

ch /sh/, /k/: We know CH spells /ch/ as in chip. CH can also spell /sh/ as in chute and /k/ as in chord.

gn /n/: We know /n/ can be spelled KN at the beginning of a word as in knot. GN also spells /n/ at the beginning of a word like gnat or at the end of a word like reign.

gh /g/: GH spells /g/ at the beginning of a word like ghost.

silent t: T is sometimes silent in the middle of a two-syllable word with the letter patterns STEN and STLE such as the words listen and whistle.

Articulatory Gesture

Review articulatory gestures for /sh/, /k/, /n/, and /g/, as needed.

Sound Wall

Add 'ch' under /k/ and under /sh/. Add 'gn' under /n/. Add 'gh' under /g/.

The School Chorus

Chris is in the school chorus. The chorus is a group of students who sing together. Chris goes to chorus practice every day after school. At chorus practice, the students learn how to sing new songs. First, everyone listens to their teacher sing the song. Next, they hum the song. Then, they whistle the tune. Finally, they all sing together. After many weeks of practice, the chorus learned eight new songs. They were now ready to sing with the orchestra. The orchestra is a group of students who play instruments together. The chorus and the orchestra met in the auditorium to perform. The music they made was beautiful. It echoed through the halls and brought joy to all who could hear it.
