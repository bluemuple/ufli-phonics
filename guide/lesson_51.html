ng /ŋ/: The grapheme NG spells /ŋ/

Instructional Notes

NG spells one sound, /ŋ/, at the end of a word or syllable (sing, long). It is a nasal sound made at the back of the mouth. Do not say /n/ + /g/.

5: New Concept

Introduction

Today we are learning about the letters NG. N and G together spell one sound, /ŋ/, like at the end of ring. It is a nasal sound: the air goes through your nose.

NG comes at the end of a word or syllable, such as king and long. NG never comes at the beginning of a word.

Grapheme placement (slides)

king/long/ng

Articulatory Gesture

To make the /ŋ/ sound as in 'ring', pull the back of your tongue up to the back of the roof of your mouth and let your voice hum through your nose... (model). /ŋ/ is a continuous sound. Watch me /ŋŋŋ/. You try /ŋŋŋ/.

Sound Wall

Add 'ng' under /ŋ/ (tongue pulled back).

King and Ash Sing

King and Ash are best pals. To have fun, King and Ash sing songs. King brings Ash a book of songs. “Which song should we sing?” asks King. “Let’s pick a quick song and then a long song,” said Ash. King and Ash sing their songs. King’s mum just got back from the shop. “Will you sing me a song?” she asks. “Yes!” yell Ash and King. They grab the book of songs. “We have to pick the best song for your mum,” said Ash. King looks in the book. He sees the best song. “This is it,” he says. King and Ash sing. King’s mum claps and says, “I am so glad you sang that song.”
