-ed: The suffix -ed

Instructional Notes

-ed makes a verb past tense. It spells three sounds: /t/ after quiet sounds (jumped), /d/ after voiced sounds (called), /əd/ after t or d (rested, landed). Students spell -ed for all three.

Today's words are base words that do not change spelling. The doubling rule (stop → stopped) and drop-e rule come in Lessons 107 and 109.

5: New Concept

Introduction

Today we will learn the suffix -ed. When we add -ed to an action word (a verb), it means the action already happened - in the past. Today I jump. Yesterday I jumped.

-ed can sound three ways. After quiet sounds it says /t/: jumped, asked. After noisy sounds it says /d/: yelled, called. After /t/ or /d/ it says /əd/ and adds a syllable: rested, handed.

No matter how it sounds, we spell it e-d. When you read a word that ends in -ed, cover the -ed, read the base word, then add the ending.

Articulatory Gesture

Compare /t/ (voice off) and /d/ (voice on) with a hand on the throat.

Sound Wall

Add '-ed' under /t/, /d/ and /əd/.

Decodable Text (from the slides)

Will the Whale

Once upon a time there was a whale named Will. Will lived with many whales in a pod. Will likes his pod. The pod splashed and jumped in the waves. The pod drifted from place to place. One time, Will and his pod of whales ended up in a cove. The cove was filled with fish and krill. The fish swam in the waves. The krill drifted with the tide. Will and his pod of whales flicked their fins to splash the fish. The fish flicked their fins back at the whales. The whales and the fish did not splash any of the krill. And the krill did not splash any of the whales or the fish. The whales and the fish had fun while the krill just drifted.
