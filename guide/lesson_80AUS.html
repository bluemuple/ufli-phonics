5: New Concept

Introduction

We have been learning that the /er/ sound is an r-controlled vowel sound. ER can come in the middle of a word, such as the words verb and fern. ER can also come at the end of a word, such as the word her.

However, when we see ER at the end of a two-syllable word, it is usually pronounced using the schwa sound, /ə/, such as the words letter and number.

Grapheme placement (slides)

verb/fern/er ; her/er

Articulatory Gesture

To make the schwa /ə/ sound, open your mouth just a little bit, like this... (model). Be sure your voice is on. Unlike all the other vowel sounds, we don't really stretch out the schwa. We say it more quickly. Watch me /ə/. You try /ə/. Sometimes, a schwa can sound a little like a short u /ŭ/ or a short i /ĭ/, but it's actually more relaxed than either of those. Watch me again /ə/. You try again /ə/.

Sound Wall

Add 'er' under /ə/.

Letter Formation

Practise letter formation, as needed.

Decodable Text (from the slides)

The Rafting Trip

Amber and her father went on a winter rafting trip. They packed up their car and drove to the river. The staff at the riverside shop gave them a map and some very helpful tips. “The water will be cold and fast, so you better hold on,” they said. They entered the rapids. Amber gripped her paddle and asked her father to do the same. She did not want to tip over or go under the water. Amber saw some litter. “We better pick up the litter,” she said. At the end of the river, Amber and her father docked their raft on the riverbank. Together they walked back to the car and had some snacks.
