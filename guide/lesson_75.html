-le: The final stable syllable -le

Instructional Notes

-le is a final stable syllable: consonant + le at the end of a word (ap-ple, puz-zle). Divide before the consonant that comes before -le. If the first syllable is closed, the vowel is short (lit-tle); if open, it is long (ta-ble).

5: New Concept

Introduction

A syllable is a word or word part with one vowel sound. We know closed syllables (short vowel) and open syllables (long vowel). Today we will learn a final stable syllable: -le.

Final means it comes last. Stable means it always looks and sounds the same. -le comes at the end of a word with a consonant in front of it: ap-ple, puz-zle. The -le syllable says /əl/ - the e is silent and the L makes the sound.

To read a word ending in -le, divide before the consonant that comes before -le: lit / tle, ta / ble. Read the first syllable, then add the -le syllable.

Articulatory Gesture

Say the /əl/ syllable: a quick relaxed /ə/ then /l/.

Maple Fudge

Uncle Billy wanted to take Able to the candy shop. They drove to a lot and jumped on a shuttle to the shop. When they walked into the candy shop, they smelled the fudge on the table. Able wanted a sample of the maple fudge. He hoped it would not be a hassle for the woman behind the table. “Can I have a sample?” asked Able. “Yes, you can! Which would you like to sample?” said the woman. Able, with a giggle, asked for the maple fudge. “You picked the best one to sample!” said the woman. Able nibbled on the fudge. “This is so yummy!” he said while licking his lips. Uncle Billy got a little bag of fudge for Able to take back home.
