ch /ch/: The consonant digraph CH spells /ch/

Instructional Notes

CH spells /ch/ at the beginning of a word (chip) and at the end after a consonant or vowel (much, bench, rich). After a short vowel at the end of a word the /ch/ is usually spelled TCH - Lesson 69. For now, students read rich, much, such as they appear.

5: New Concept

Introduction

We know that a consonant digraph is two consonants that come together to make one sound, like SH and TH. Today we will learn the digraph CH. C and H together make a new sound, /ch/, like at the beginning of chicken.

CH can come at the beginning of a word, such as chip and chin. CH can also come at the end of a word, such as such and rich, and after N, such as bench and lunch.

Grapheme placement (slides)

chip/chin/ch ; such/rich/ch

Articulatory Gesture

To make the /ch/ sound, put your teeth together and round your lips, then pop the air out with your voice off... (model). /ch/ is a quick sound. It is like /j/ without the voice. Watch me /ch/. You try /ch/.

Sound Wall

Add 'ch' under /ch/ (tongue lifted).

Letter Formation

Practise letter formation for ch.
