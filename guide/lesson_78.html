or, ore /or/: The graphemes OR and ORE spell /or/

Instructional Notes

OR spells /or/ at the beginning, middle or end of a word (orb, fork, for). ORE spells /or/ at the end of a word (more, store). In Australian/NZ English /or/ is a long vowel with no /r/ sound.

5: New Concept

Introduction

Today we are learning another r-controlled vowel. When O is followed by R, the R changes the vowel sound. OR spells /or/, like in fork and corn.

OR can come at the beginning of a word (orb), in the middle (fork, corn) or at the end (for). At the end of a word, /or/ is often spelled ORE, with a silent e: more, store, shore.

Grapheme placement (slides)

Articulatory Gesture

To make the /or/ sound as in 'orange', round your lips and open your mouth a little... (model). Be sure your voice is on. /or/ is a continuous sound. Watch me /or/. You try /or/.

Marvel at the Shore

Cory and his horse, North, went to the shore. Cory held on to North’s mane as North galloped across the sand. They arrived at the shore before anyone else. There had been a storm at sunset, so this morning the shore was a marvel. There were hundreds of starfish along the shoreline. The starfish got pulled to the shore in the storm. They were so pretty, but if they were left on the shore they would dry up. Cory jumped off North’s back. He picked up starfish one by one and placed them back where they came from. Cory was happy he could transport the starfish back home.
