Doubling Rule -er, -est: Doubling rule with -er and -est

Instructional Notes

The same 1-1-1 doubling rule applies to -er and -est: big → bigger, biggest; hot → hotter, hottest. Do not double after two consonants or a vowel team (fast → faster; cool → cooler).

5: New Concept

Introduction

We know that -er means 'more than' and -est means 'the most'. Yesterday we learned the doubling rule for -ed and -ing. The same rule works for -er and -est.

Look at big: b-i-g. One vowel, one final consonant - a 1-1-1 word. To add -er or -est, double the last consonant: bigger, biggest. The double g keeps the i short.

If the word ends in two consonants or has a vowel team, do not double: fast → faster, fastest; cool → cooler, coolest.

The Hottest Day

One August day, Tyler went outside to play tag with his friends. The sun was high in the sky. It felt like the hottest day of the year. As Tyler was running around, his face got redder and redder and he felt hotter and hotter. “It’s too hot outside to play tag,” Tyler called to his friends, “let’s go swimming instead.” Tyler and his friends hopped on their bikes and rode to the pool. At the pool, they had a contest to see who could make the biggest splash in the water. Tyler made a pretty big splash, but his friends made bigger ones. The friends had lots of fun at the pool. The saddest part of the day was when they had to stop playing and go back home.
